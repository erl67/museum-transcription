COLLECTION OF
H. W. and C. M. BRANDT
A. O. U. No. 355
Date April 11 1920
Set Mark 1/5
Species: Prairie Falcon [and] Falco mexicanus Calif.
Locality: San Juan River 17 miles above Shandon, San Luis Obispo [Co.]
Character of Country: Rolling open treeless cow country
Identification: By photo.
Incubation: One week
Weather: Clear and hot -
No. of Eggs in Nest: 5
Method of Travel: We motored up the San Juan River 17 miles over good roads and then hiked 1/4 mile into the hills.
Companions: Fred Tuesdale and Nel Carpenter
Altitude: 1400.
Size of Eggs: -
Set Quality: A1
Collector: [J. W.] Brandt
Nest: Diameter: Inside 10 inches
Nest: Diameter: Outside 18 inches
Depth: Inside on sand inches
Depth: Outside 4 inches
Data: Nesting in an old Ravens nest built 3 years ago in a pot hole that was entirely filled by the nest. It was in a high sandstone cliff up 20 feet from the bottom of a steep talus slope and 110 feet from the top of the cliff. Used 100 feet of rope and 30 feet of steel ladder. The nest was overhung and hard to reach. The inside of the Ravens nest had been filled with sand from the roof so that it nearly filled it. On this the eggs were placed. As we came up the incubating bird crawled up on the edge of the nest and calmly posed for the camera, as it flew about it called sexactly as a Duck Hawk with a cold, differing from the former as a crows call does from a Raven. Both birds were present. Strange to say a Raven usually nests near to a Falcon's, and the nest of the latter is usually in an old Ravens nest. They are in steep cliffs that break out of the rolling hills of a treeless cow pasture country.
