Set No. 5175
Species: Prairie Falcon
Falco mexicanus [Calif]
Locality: San Juan River 17 miles above Shandon, San Luis Obispo Co.
Character of Country: Rolling open treeless cow country
Identification: By photo.
Incubation: one week
Weather: Clear and hot -
No. of Eggs in Nest: 5
Method of Travel: We motors up the San Juan River 17 miles over good roads and then hike 1/4 mile into the hills.
Companions: Fred Truesdale and Nel Carpenter.
Altitude: 1400 -
Size of Eggs: -
Set Quality: A1
Collector: [H. W. Brandt]
Nest: Diameter: Inside: 10 inches
Outside: 18 inches
Depth: Inside: on sand inches
Outside: 4 inches
Data: Nesting in an old Ravens nest built 3 years ago in a pot hole that was entirely filled by the nest. It was in a high sandstone cliff up 20 feet from the bottom of a steep talus slope and 110' from the top of the cliff. Used 100' of rope and 30' of steel ladder. The nest was overhung and hard to reach. The inside of the Ravens nest had been filled with sand from the roof so that it nearly filled it. On this the eggs were placed. As we came up the incubating bird crawled up on the edge of the nest and calmly posed for the camera, as it flew about it called sexactly [sic] as a Duck Hawk with a cold, differing from the former as a crows call does from a Raven. Both birds were present. Strange to say a Raven usually nests near by to a Falcon's, and the nest of the latter is usually in an old Ravens nest. They are in steep cliffs that break out of the rolling hills of a treeless cow pasture country.
