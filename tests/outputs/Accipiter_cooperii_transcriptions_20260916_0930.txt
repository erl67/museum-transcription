H. W. and C. M. BRANDT
Species: Coopers Hawk
accipiter cooperi.
Locality: Crescent Valley, Lake Hodges, San Diego Co. Calif.
Character of Country: In a dense grove in a canyon bottom
Identification: Flushed -
Weather: Clear.
Method of Travel: We were returning with a set of Canyon Wren and were passing through a densely oak covered grove.
Companions: N. K. Carpenter
Altitude: 350
Size of Eggs: -
Set Quality: A1
Collector: H W Brandt
Nest: Diameter: Inside 7 inches
Nest: Diameter: Outside 24 inches
Depth: Inside 3 inches
Depth: Outside 12 inches
Data: I located this nest by throwing a stick against a limb supporting it and flushing the incubating bird. Previously the bird sat tightly when Hels club the tree. The oaks here grew large and were close together so that their tops intertwined in a solid mat with many large rat nests and leaf mats. The nest was an old structure against the trunk up 35 feet in a medium sized tree. It was a large nest and well covered with whitish feathers. The lining was triangular chips of sycamore bark and green oak leaves. These leaves stain the eggs so that they often looked blotched. The bird made a great fuss crying "cae! cae!" sounding not unlike a coot from back in the grove and I took it for a marsh hawk at first. A Barn owl flushed and the hawks went after it at a great rate clawing up the owl quite badly. As we were driving away they were still calling.
